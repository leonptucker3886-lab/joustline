# Joustline: Comedy and Time Travelers
The tone is a straight historical drama that keeps getting interrupted by nonsense. History stays real, and the jokes happen in the margins, like the doodles monks drew in manuscripts.

## Running gags
- **Tancrède the horse** has opinions and shows them with side-eye cutaways. He refuses to gallop toward anyone wearing yellow.
- **Old Gaspard** narrates the tutorial and keeps getting the history wrong. Bayard (from Chapter 2) corrects him every time.
- **Marginalia:** snails in armor and rabbits fighting knights pop up in the corners and comment on your bad hits.
- **Failure screens** are royal decrees, like "His Majesty has seen worse. Not often."
- Leonardo's armor designs keep adding a helicopter. "Next century, perhaps."

## The time travelers
They show up at every chapter's big event. Courtiers can't see them unless you have the "Second Sight" trinket.

- **The Cartoonist** (wink): a lanky man from the future with glasses, a goatee and a sketchbook. He draws the French court as yellow-tinted caricatures with overbites. He calls Francis I "a total dough-fin," mutters "ay, caramelo" when you fall off, and tries to sell Leonardo a show about a nuclear plant. His sketchbook is a side quest: find his 12 lost drawings, each a yellow-tinted version of a real courtier. Reward: the cosmetic "Pastry Crest", a gold ring-cake on a field of blue. He's never named.
- **The Tourist**: wears cargo shorts and a "Cloth of Gold 1520 Tour" T-shirt, and spoils the Pavia outcome to everyone.
- **The Inspector** (joke ending): a future man in a hard hat who condemns Château de Sampsonne for "zero fire exits."
- **The Influencer**: livestreams the Field of the Cloth of Gold on a phone with no signal.
- **The Historian**: frantically tells you not to change 1559. Ignore him and you're on the save-Henri path, which feeds the Sampsonne battle.
